Product Lifecycle (PLC)

What is a Product Lifecycle?

A Product Lifecycle is the journey of a product from its launch to its eventual decline or retirement.

Idea → Launch → Growth → Maturity → Decline
Stage 1
Development

Research, prototyping, testing. No revenue yet — only investment.

Stage 2
Introduction

Product launched. Low awareness, slow sales, high marketing costs.

Stage 3
Growth

Sales grow rapidly. Competitors enter. Focus on scaling.

Stage 4
Maturity

Growth slows, market saturated. Revenue at peak. Intense competition.

Stage 5
Decline

Demand drops. New tech replaces product. Revamp or sunset.

PM Focus at Each Stage

StagePM FocusKey Metric
IntroductionProduct-market fit, user feedbackAcquisition, Activation
GrowthScaling, feature improvement, retentionRetention, Engagement
MaturityOptimization, differentiation, loyaltyRevenue, Profitability
DeclineRevamp or sunset decisionCost Optimization
🎤 30-Second Interview Answer

"The Product Lifecycle describes the stages a product goes through from development to retirement: Development, Introduction, Growth, Maturity, and Decline. Each stage requires different PM strategies — early stages focus on product-market fit and adoption; maturity focuses on optimization and retention; decline involves deciding whether to reinvent or sunset the product."

Value in Product Management

What is value, why is it important, and how is it calculated?
Value = Benefits Received − Costs Incurred

A PM's job is to create value for customers, the business, and stakeholders — not just to build features.

Who Receives Value?

TypeBenefitsExample
Customer ValueSaves time, money, effortFood delivery saves travel
Business ValueRevenue, market share, retentionSubscriptions increase recurring revenue
Stakeholder ValueBrand reputation, complianceInvestor confidence grows
Technical ValueReduced debt, better scalabilityModern architecture = faster deploys

How to Calculate Value

Method 1 — Cost-Benefit Analysis

Value = Benefits − Costs Example: Revenue ₹10L − Dev Cost ₹3L = ₹7L value

Method 2 — ROI

ROI = (Benefit − Cost) / Cost × 100 Example: (₹50L − ₹10L) / ₹10L × 100 = 400%

Method 3 — RICE Framework

RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort

Method 4 — Opportunity Scoring

Value = Importance − Satisfaction If Importance = 9/10 and Satisfaction = 3/10 → Opportunity = 6
🎤 Interview Answer

"Value in PM is the measurable benefit delivered to customers, the business, and stakeholders. PMs define value by understanding customer pain points, business objectives, and strategic goals. Value can be measured through Cost-Benefit Analysis, ROI, RICE scoring, and outcome metrics like retention and satisfaction."

Value Management

What is Value Management?

Value Management is the process of identifying, measuring, prioritizing, delivering, and continuously improving the value created by a product or investment.

Value Management Process: 1. Identify Value → Understand customer pain points & business goals 2. Measure Value → Define success metrics (revenue, retention, CSAT) 3. Prioritize Value → Use RICE, ROI, MoSCoW, Cost-Benefit 4. Deliver Value → Build MVP, iterate 5. Track Value → Verify value was delivered post-launch 6. Optimize Value → Continuously improve based on data

PM's 5 Key Questions

  1. What value are we creating?
  2. For whom are we creating it?
  3. How will we measure it?
  4. Is the value worth the investment?
  5. Did we actually deliver the expected value?
💡 A value-focused PM separates themselves from a feature-focused PM. Always ask "Should we build this?" not just "Can we build this?"
🎤 Interview Answer

"Value Management is the practice of maximizing the value delivered to customers, the business, and stakeholders while optimizing resources like time, budget, and engineering effort. It involves identifying value opportunities, prioritizing them, measuring outcomes, and continuously improving based on data."

Understanding the Customer

Who is the customer? What problem do they have? What do they want? How can you solve it?

These are the four fundamental questions every PM must continuously ask.

1. Who is the Customer?

Understand: demographics, behaviors, goals, motivations, pain points, and buying power. Use user interviews, surveys, support tickets, analytics, and personas.

2. What is the Underlying Problem?

Customers describe symptoms, not root causes. Use the 5 Whys method.

Customer says: "Food delivery takes too long." Why? → Riders delayed → Why? → Routes inefficient → Why? → No traffic data → Root: Poor route optimization

3. What Does the Customer Want?

Customer SaysCustomer Actually Wants
Faster horseFaster transportation
Dark modeComfortable night viewing
More reportsBetter decision-making
More notificationsOnly the important updates

4. How Can We Solve It?

Generate options → Evaluate (customer value, business value, effort, risk) → Build MVP → Measure → Iterate.

PM Thinking Framework: Customer → Problem → Need → Solution → MVP → Metrics → Learn & Improve

Capturing Value

Who will pay? How much? What is the market value? Is the problem solved?

Value Capture is converting customer value into revenue, profit, or business growth.

Key Questions for Value Capture

QuestionInsight
Who will pay?User ≠ Payer (e.g. Slack: employees use, company pays)
How much will they pay?Depends on problem severity, frequency, ROI delivered
What pricing strategy?Cost-based, Competitor-based, or Value-based (best for SaaS)
Is problem solved?Track before/after metrics: checkout time, conversion, engagement
What is market value?TAM × SAM × SOM analysis

Market Size Formula

Market Value = Number of Customers × Average Revenue Per Customer Example: 1,000,000 customers × ₹2,000/year = ₹200 Crore opportunity
Full PM Value Loop: Customer → Problem → Need → Solution → Value Creation → Value Capture → Revenue → Growth → Business Success

Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

What is an MVP?
MVP = Minimum Features + Maximum Learning

The simplest version of a product that delivers core value and validates assumptions with real users.

MVP vs Prototype vs POC

TypePurpose
POC (Proof of Concept)Checks if something is technically feasible
PrototypeDemonstrates design and user flow (Figma)
MVPReal product released to users to validate assumptions

How PMs Build an MVP

  1. Identify the core user problem
  2. Define the minimum core value to deliver
  3. List all possible features
  4. Ruthlessly cut to must-haves only
  5. Launch and measure (adoption, retention, conversion)
Golden Rule: Build → Measure → Learn → Iterate
🎤 Interview Answer

"An MVP is the simplest version of a product that delivers core value to users while enabling teams to validate assumptions and gather feedback with minimal investment. The objective is to learn quickly, iterate based on data, and avoid building features that users may not need."

A/B Testing

What is A/B Testing?
A/B Testing = Compare → Measure → Decide

An experiment where two versions of a feature are shown to different user groups and measured to see which performs better.

A/B Testing Process

  1. Define a hypothesis ("Moving mic button to center will increase voice adoption")
  2. Choose success metrics (conversion rate, CTR, retention)
  3. Split users randomly (50/50 or other split)
  4. Run the experiment long enough for statistical significance
  5. Analyze results and roll out the winner

When to Use A/B Testing

✅ Use For❌ Avoid When
UI changes, pricing, onboarding flowsUser base too small
New features, notificationsChanges impact security/compliance
Recommendation algorithmsExperiment could harm users
🎤 Interview Answer

"A/B testing is an experimentation method where users are randomly split and exposed to different versions of a feature. The goal is to compare predefined metrics and make data-driven decisions based on statistically significant results."

North Star Metric, Input, Guardrail & Vanity Metrics

What are North Star, Input, Guardrail, and Vanity Metrics?
Metric TypeDefinitionExample
North StarSingle metric that best reflects core product valueNetflix: Watch time | Uber: Completed rides
Input MetricsMetrics that drive the North StarUber: Driver availability, Payment success rate
Guardrail MetricsMetrics that must NOT worsen during optimizationChurn rate, CSAT, Crash rate
Vanity MetricsLook impressive but don't indicate real valueTotal downloads, Social media likes

Interview Framework — When Asked About Metrics

  1. Define the North Star Metric (what value does the product create?)
  2. Define Input Metrics (what actions drive that value?)
  3. Define Guardrail Metrics (what must not degrade?)
  4. Identify Vanity Metrics (what looks good but doesn't reflect real success?)

Example: Food Delivery App

TypeMetric
North StarOrders Delivered
InputActive Users, Restaurant Availability, Checkout Conversion, Payment Success
GuardrailDelivery Time, Customer Ratings, Refund Rate
VanityApp Downloads, Social Followers, Website Visits

How to Deal with Failure as a PM

How do you deal with failure as a PM?

Failure is inevitable in PM. A great PM fails fast, learns quickly, and improves continuously.

Framework: Failure → Analysis → Learning → Iteration → Growth

Steps to Handle Failure

  1. Acknowledge — Accept the outcome objectively without blaming others
  2. Measure the Gap — Compare expected vs actual KPIs
  3. Root Cause Analysis — Use 5 Whys to find the real reason
  4. Gather Feedback — User interviews, surveys, support tickets, session recordings
  5. Iterate — Fix UI, change onboarding, reposition, or roll back
  6. Share Learnings — Communicate transparently with stakeholders
🎤 Interview Answer

"I view failure as a learning opportunity. I assess impact using KPIs, conduct root cause analysis using data and customer feedback, prioritize corrective actions, iterate on the solution, and communicate transparently with stakeholders. My focus is on learning quickly and continuously improving the product."
